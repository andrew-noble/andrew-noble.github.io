A Philosophy of Walking

Author: Frederic Gros

Rating: 7

A relaxing collection of essays about walking and the role it played in famous philosophers' and liberators' lives. My favorite chapters are "Eternities," "Walking as Caressing," and "States of Well-Being." Favorite idea is when author drew distinctions between pleasure, joy, happiness as states of well being. See p.122.

Bad Blood

Author: John Carreyrou

Rating: 4

A highly journalistic piece of reportage on the rise and fall of biomedical startup Theranos and its charismatic leader, Elizabeth Holmes.

There's a lesson embedded in the fact that the whistleblowers were all recent college grads, and supports the idea that people naturally question themselves less as they get older. George Shultz and James Mattis were none the wiser, but Tyler Shultz (GS's grandson), a recent Stanford grad, was the one to crack the facade.

I think this story is basically indistinguishable from cult stories like The People's Temple or Heaven's Gate-- all the same techniques were used.

Fear and Loathing in Las Vegas

Author: Hunter S. Thompson

Rating: 9

A hilarious and shockingly told story about a 5-day bender in Las Vegas. I've never LOL'ed so much in a book.

The book tries to make some vague statements about the waning culture of the 1960's, but to me it was mostly just a frenzied drug story (but a very entertaining one). Paranoia and a journalistic stoicness (almost Kafkaesque, honestly) in the face of insane events were well conveyed by HST.

"Buy the ticket, take the ride...and if it occasionally gets a little heavier than what you had in mind, well...maybe chalk it up to forced consciousness expansion: Tune in, freak out, get beaten."

Of Mice and Men

Author: John Steinbeck

Rating: 7

We see twice in this book men having to part with a companion. Carlson coerces Candy into letting him kill his ailing dog. Then later we have the climax event of George mercykilling Lennie.

Again and again in this book, the concept of loneliness and needing other people is brought up. Curly's wife feels lonely, all the itinerant farmworkers feel alone. Some find relief (Candy with his dog, arguably George with Lennie), others don't and are bitter (Crooks).

Crooks' cynicism around George and Lennie's farmstead fantasies remind me of Red's dismissal of Andy's escape "pipe dream" in [[The Shawshank Redeption]]. In this book, though, there is no happy ending.

The Dispossessed

Author: Ursula K. LeGuin

Rating: 6

A layered book that juxtaposes a capitalist, propertarian society (Urras) with an anarcho-communist one. Flaws are explained in both, with the capitalist society having deeper flaws ultimately. Also a story about social responsibility and how being responsible for one another may actually be freeing, counterintuitively.

The Lathe of Heaven

Author: Ursula K. LeGuin

Rating: 3

An just okay book about the consequences of playing God. Similar message as Brave New World. The "problems" of life, like war, famine, suffering, disease-- are inherent to it. To fix them is not to ascend to a higher existence, but to cease existing altogether.

The Left Hand of Darkness

Author: Ursula K. LeGuin

Rating: 9

A great read that brings gender roles, nationalism, power, and alien-ness into relief through a world of ambisexual humans called Gethenians. It's a "what if" book (UKL's specialty) about how deeply impactful gender is on who we are and also how vastly different men and women are.

It uses gender at the lens, but more generally this story is about empathy and trying to cross the space that lies between each of us. [[The Empathy Gulf]]

A really excellent one that lived rent free in my head all of 2024. The big message in here is how epistemically alone we are. There's no real knowing of another, and they can't really know you in any meaningful way. Nevertheless, fruitlessly trying to cross that gap is what we call empathy.

The Prince

Author: Machiavelli

Rating: 9

An amazing book. Machiavelli's ideas are founded on a stark view of humans-- seeing them as they are and not how you'd like them to be. Human flaws exist whether we like it or not, and if you want to make your way in the world, you need to be aware of these flaws.

If I had to pick the most prominent theme, it would be decisiveness. It shines through most of Machiavelli's advice. Decisiveness + boldness = confidence.

Think

Author: Simon Blackburn

Rating: 3

This book isn't well written, in my opinion. The concepts aren't that complex but it just wasn't written very clearly, with especially inadequate delineation between each view on each problem. Nevertheless, a decent, broad survey.

Favorite ideas, mostly for the imagery, was Descarte's "Sea of Doubt" and his Cartesian Demon. Also: compatibilist vs. strict determinism and the entire ethics section. I think I prefer normative philosophy (what to do) more than theoretical philosophy (how things are).

This is Water

Author: David Foster Wallace

Rating: 9

This is actually a commencement speech, not a book. It's very profound.

DFW makes the argument that the central purpose of education and really life in general is to decide what to pay attention to. Our default attention setting is one of selfishness that makes us miserable. By getting out of your own head and considering others' perspective, you come closer to seeing real truth. Very implicated in the ideas in The Left Hand of Darkness by UKL.

Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance

Author: Robert M. Pirsig

Rating: 9

I failed to get through this book in college. Rereading it years later, its much better. Pirsig tries to tackle an enormous problem of philosophy; he's not quite successful, but I suspect there are additional layers to discover on re-reads.

Also, encounters with the Ultimate ("Quality") begin with emotional, feeling experience and quickly transition to slower, thinking experience thus revealing feeling/thinking divide. His analogy of a train for this, rationality being towed along by the heart, is very memorable.

Curiosity, I think, is the perfect word to describe this train. It's both feeling and thinking-- it's the love of thought.
